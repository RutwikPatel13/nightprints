# Printer handoff checklist

For your friend, before the first order. Most of these are one question to the print shop. Getting them answered before printing avoids the usual surprises: wrong repeat size, washed-out colors, or a file the printer can't use.

## Ask the printer

**Printing method.** Digital (direct-to-fabric) or sublimation? Sublimation only works on polyester or poly blends and gives brighter color. Cotton nightwear is usually printed digitally, where colors come out a little softer.

**File format.** Most accept PNG or TIFF. Ask whether they want RGB (what nightprints exports) or need CMYK. If they need CMYK, ask them to convert it, since they know their machine's color profile.

**Resolution.** nightprints exports at 300 DPI. Many fabric printers are fine with 150 DPI. Ask so you don't send files bigger than they need.

**Repeat size.** Ask the maximum repeat they support and what size they recommend for kids sleepwear. The default tile is about 13.6 inches square at 300 DPI. Smaller motifs suit toddler sizes, larger ones suit ages 6 to 10. The app can export a smaller repeat if needed.

**Repeat type.** Straight (block) repeat or half-drop? nightprints tiles are straight repeats. Half-drop needs a different setup, so confirm.

**Fabric width.** Usually 44, 54 or 58 inches. This tells you how many repeats fit across.

**Color matching.** Do they offer a color strike-off, a small printed sample of your colors on the actual fabric? Ask what it costs and how long it takes.

**Minimum order and turnaround,** per design and per color way.

## Before the first real order

- **Order a test swatch of 2 or 3 designs on the exact fabric.** Wash it once. Check that colors still look right, the pattern lines up at the edges, and nothing looks blurry or pixelated up close.
- **Check the seam on the swatch.** If you can see a line where the tile repeats, send me the file and I'll check its seam score.
- **Look at every final design at 100% zoom** for AI mistakes: extra legs, merged shapes, half-formed stars. Fix or drop anything off.
- **Confirm sleepwear safety rules with the printer or fabric supplier.** Children's sleepwear has fabric and flammability requirements in the US and many other countries. Those rules are about the fabric and garment, not the print file, but the supplier should confirm the fabric meets them.

## About owning the designs

In the US, images made purely by AI generally can't be copyrighted. Your friend can sell them, but can't stop someone else from copying them. Hand-editing the final designs (recoloring, redrawing details, combining elements) adds human authorship and makes them easier to protect. This isn't legal advice, so if it matters for the business, ask an IP lawyer.

## What to send the printer

- The `_print.png` file for each design (300 DPI, reduced palette, seamless for patterns)
- The repeat size in inches (file width divided by 300)
- Fabric choice and quantity
- The `_mockup.png` if they want to see the intended look
